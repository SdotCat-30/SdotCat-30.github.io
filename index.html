About

I investigate extreme rainfall, compound hydroclimatic events, and rain-on-snow processes using climate observations, hydrological modeling, and computational analysis.

I'm an M.S. Geoscience student and Graduate Research Assistant in the Climate Research Lab at Western Kentucky University, advised by Dr. Zachary J. Suriano. My thesis asks whether extreme rainfall in Kentucky's Salt River Basin is becoming more frequent, and how antecedent soil moisture shapes the streamflow response when it does.

Alongside the thesis, I work on hemispheric rain-on-snow events and their surface energy budgets, and on precipitation-frequency estimates from the Kentucky Mesonet. I also served as a teaching assistant for the METR 121 meteorology lab.

Before WKU, I earned a B.E. in Civil Engineering from Tribhuvan University (IOE Pulchowk Campus) and registered as a civil engineer with the Nepal Engineering Council.

Extreme rainfall in the Salt River Basin

Salt River Basin, Kentucky · Climate Research Lab, WKU

Are extreme rainfall events in the Salt River Basin becoming more frequent? I apply a non-stationary peaks-over-threshold (POT) framework to 44 years of 4-km PRISM daily precipitation. Thresholds follow the seasonal cycle (adapted from Acero et al., 2011), a basin-coverage criterion flags basin-wide events, and Ferro–Segers declustering separates independent storms. The next stage links these events to streamflow and antecedent soil moisture to study compound hydroclimatic extremes.

  • Independent extreme-rainfall events became significantly more frequent from 1981 to 2024: about +1.8 events per decade (Sen's slope; Mann–Kendall p < 0.001), against a mean of 12.8 events per year.
  • Winter, spring, and summer drive the increase. Fall shows no significant trend in frequency, but fall storms carry the largest mean excess above threshold.

Rain-on-snow events and surface energy fluxes

Northern Hemisphere · Climate Research Lab, WKU

Rain falling on snowpack can trigger rapid melt and flooding. Using hourly ERA5 reanalysis from 1980 to 2025, I identified rain-on-snow (ROS) hours across Northern Hemisphere land at three intensity thresholds (0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 mm per hour). I then examined the surface energy budget during those hours: sensible and latent heat, net solar, and net thermal radiation, by region and by elevation.

  • Built event catalogs, climatologies, and energy summaries from about 2.5 TB of hourly NetCDF data with Python, xarray, and Dask.
  • Mapped how much of the net energy during ROS comes from turbulent (sensible + latent) heat fluxes. The manuscript argues these fluxes are key to understanding extreme ROS events.

Precipitation frequency from the Kentucky Mesonet vs. NOAA Atlas 14

Kentucky · Western Kentucky University

Engineers size culverts, bridges, and storm drains with NOAA Atlas 14 precipitation-frequency estimates. We derived intensity–duration–frequency (IDF) statistics directly from high-resolution Kentucky Mesonet observations and compared them with Atlas 14 to see how well the published values hold up across durations. The team included graduate and undergraduate researchers under faculty supervision.

66 Mesonet stations · 5-min observations · 2015–2024 record · 5 min–24 h durations · 6 matched Atlas 14 station pairs

  • Found duration-dependent differences between station-based and Atlas 14 estimates, with implications for hydrologic design, flood-risk assessment, and regional climate services.
Published 2025H2Open Journal 8(6), 465–490Second author

Climate change and hydropower in the Kankai River Basin

Kankai River Basin, eastern Nepal · Tribhuvan University, IOE Pulchowk Campus

Nepal depends on run-of-river hydropower, so changes in streamflow translate directly into changes in energy supply. We forced a calibrated and validated SWAT model with bias-corrected projections from six CMIP6 global climate models under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, then estimated energy generation at three hydropower projects through 2100.

  • Annual precipitation rises by up to 42% under SSP5-8.5 by the end of the century, and streamflow follows.
  • Energy generation dips in the near future, then increases in the mid and far future, most strongly under SSP5-8.5.
  • Year-to-year swings in generation call for climate-resilient planning of run-of-river projects.

Design of a dual water supply system

Gitanagar, Nagarkot, Nepal · IOE Pulchowk Campus · Supervisor: Assoc. Prof. Shukra Raj Paudel

We designed a combined pumping and gravity-fed water supply system for a 20-year design period, from field surveys, population projections, and demand assessment through to intake, treatment, storage, and distribution.

  • Designed sedimentation, rapid sand filtration, and chlorination units and storage reservoirs.
  • Modeled the distribution network in WaterGEMS and prepared drawings in AutoCAD and ArcGIS.
  • Estimated a project cost of about NPR 43.1 million.
